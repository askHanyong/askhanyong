By Hanyong, former IB Examiner and DP Coordinator

Why Grade Boundaries Matter

Your final IB grade—that all-important 1 to 7—isn't calculated directly from your raw marks. Instead, the IB sets a grade boundary each exam session. This boundary translates your total weighted marks out of 100 into your grade. The catch? These boundaries change every session based on how the cohort performed.

This post pulls data from the official IB Mathematics AA grade boundary documents from May 2021 through May 2026. I've marked IB scripts as an examiner, so I've seen firsthand how these numbers get set—and what they really mean for your performance.

All numbers below are directly from IB published data. No rounding, no estimates. The patterns are real.

How Your Grade is Worked Out

Your final IB grade is based on a weighted total out of 100 marks:

Higher Level (HL)

Paper 1: 30% (110 marks) + Paper 2: 30% (110 marks) + Paper 3: 20% (55 marks) + Internal Assessment: 20% (20 marks)

Standard Level (SL)

Paper 1: 40% (80 marks) + Paper 2: 40% (80 marks) + Internal Assessment: 20% (20 marks)

Your weighted total out of 100 is then compared against the Overall grade boundary. A score of 70 out of 100 might be a 7 in May 2023 but a 6 in another session. The boundary moves because the cohort changes.

Key Takeaways from the Data

November is consistently harder. SL students targeting a 7 in November need 80% in every recent session (2023–2025). In May, the same boundary ranges from 65–79%. HL shows a similar gap: November sits around 78–79%, May around 70–76%.
HL grade 7 in May has stabilised. Since 2024, May HL boundaries have clustered around 71–76%. This is more predictable than 2021–2023, which were COVID-adjusted and volatile.
The IA is the most stable component. Grade 7 on the IA: 17/20 at HL, 18/20 at SL. This has never changed across all 11 sessions. If you secure a strong IA (17+), you're locking in marks you can predict—one less variable when you plan your paper goals.
2021 and 2022 were different. The IB explicitly noted these as COVID-adjusted sessions. Don't treat a 63/100 (May 2021 HL) as your target when recent May sessions sit at 70+. Use 2023 onwards as your benchmark.
Timezones matter, but not drastically. The boundaries do vary by timezone (sometimes by 2–4 marks), but the pattern is consistent: you can predict your target from the overall session trend, then adjust slightly for your timezone.

Aim High

Grade boundaries are a guide, not a target. The IB doesn't pre-set them to hit a specific score; they emerge from how the cohort performed. Aim above the boundary. If a grade 7 typically sits at 70%, target 75–80% to give yourself a buffer for exam day nerves or trickier question papers.
